Setup Analysis — the follow-up, with named trades

Answers to the review questions on the first analysis, backed by specific trades. Re-run against 213 analyzable closed trades (needs full 3-month price history before entry + 20 trading days after). "Worked" = a ≥ 10% favourable move in the underlying within 20 trading days of entry. All setup_score values are the current intent-aware v2 score — the original doc quoted the older v1, which is why some numbers shifted.

Read this first — the terms

Range position — where the stock sat inside its high–low range over the ~3 months (63 trading days) before entry. 0% = at the 3-month low, 100% = at the 3-month high. For puts it's flipped. "Upper range" = bought near recent highs; "low in the range" = bought closer to recent lows.

Reversal / Continuation / Chop — reversal = range ≤ 35% and below the 20-day average (beaten down). continuation = range ≥ 55%, above the 20-day average, and beating SPY over 20 days (with the trend, near highs). chop = everything else — a residual bucket, not a real category. "Chop" ≈ no clear directional posture at entry; its ~55% / −$3 is just the base rate.

"40% off the low" — off the 3-month low (lowest close in the ~63 trading days before entry), not the 52-week low.

Every one of the 5 reversal losses was cut in ≤ 1 day, and 4 of 5 still had a +10–28% move coming. The reversal book's failures are an execution problem, not a setup problem.

Will the 78% hold as sample size grows?

Maybe not. 18/23 = 78%, but the 95% confidence interval is ~61%–95% (small n). Split chronologically: first half 7/11 (64%), second half 11/12 (92%) — it has improved, but that's 12 trades. The "deep" subset (range ≤ 15%): 16/18 = 89%.

Plan around ~65% and rising with size, not 78%. Even the lower bound (61%) beats continuation (56%), so it's probably a real edge — just don't bank the headline.

5

"Contract length is the continuation lever" — you were right to push back

Re-run with the fuller data, the entry charts are not identical:

continuation subsetnworkedavg P&L20d returnoff 3m-lowRS vs SPYscore
short-dated  (≤ 65 DTE)6857%−$4+19%+56%+162.2
long-dated  (≥ 85 DTE)1932%−$38+32%+59%+270.8

The long-DTE continuation trades were bought more extended (+32% vs +19% over 20 days), with higher relative strength (chasing a hotter move: +27 vs +16), and a lower setup score (0.8 vs 2.2). Contract length isn't the cause — it's a tell that you bought later into a run. Consistent with your own read: you reach for a longer contract when less sure of the timing, and "less sure" correlates with a worse entry.

Also: within continuation, winners held longer than losers (6.2d vs 4.4d), and winners' contracts averaged 58 DTE vs losers' 92. Nothing here says "hold to expiry" — it says the trades that gapped against you early were the ones you'd bought a long contract for and bailed on fastest.

The rule

Not "buy 2-month contracts." It's: don't initiate a continuation trade on a move already run > ~25% over 20 days or > 40% off its 3-month low — and if you do, size it small, because the downside is asymmetric (next section).

6

"Avoid stocks up 40% off the low" — refined

"worked" ≠ "won"

worked means a ≥ 10% favourable move was available within 20 trading days — the thesis was directionally right. It does not mean the trade made money. Across the 40%+ group, worked = 67% but only 33% were actually P&L-positive. The 34-point gap is the move happening after you'd already exited (early, or at a loss on the first dip), or the option decaying past your exit.

Time to develop — confirmed, a bit stronger

Of the 123 trades where a ≥ 10% move was available, when — in trading days after entry — did the underlying reach its best favourable point:

The last column is 100 − share — "of all the winning moves, what fraction hadn't peaked yet." So 65% of winning moves were still climbing after trading-day 10 — only 35% had topped out.

Median time to the peak: 14 trading days (mean 13.2). ~2/3 of winning moves take longer than 10 trading days. By bucket: reversal median 18 td, continuation and chop median 14 td.

Takeaway

Allowing 15 trading days (~3 calendar weeks) for a trade to develop is right; reversal trades need closer to 4 weeks. A 2-day exit rule is structurally incompatible with this.

8

Counterfactual — holding the ≤ 2-day exits longer

Method: each trade cut in ≤ 2 days is re-priced with Black–Scholes. Back out the implied vol from the actual entry premium, then re-value the same contract at a later day using the underlying's real close, reduced days-to-expiry, and (realistic runs) a −15% IV haircut on calls, since a stock that rallies 20–40% almost always sees its option IV drop. Absurd back-out IVs (> 160%) excluded. P&L is net of actual commissions.

8b · Realistic — replace the ≤ 2-day rule with a fixed hold on every quick-cut

Winners and losers held longer, BS re-price with the −15% call-IV haircut:

  • A 10-day hold barely moves the needle — the extra losses roughly cancel the extra wins.
  • A 15-day hold flips the account from −$824 to +$1,240 and profit factor 0.96 → 1.14.
  • A 20-day hold does better still (+$4,170, PF 1.35).
Why longer holds win here

A losing option is capped at −100% (you can't lose more than the premium); a winning one can run +100–300%. Hold everything longer and the extra downside is bounded while the extra upside isn't. That asymmetry is the whole case for a minimum-hold rule — you don't need to pick the winners, just stop cutting them.

Caveats: constant-IV would be too generous; the −15% call haircut is a rough correction. Assumes you could carry 113 positions at once — capital/slots would cap that. Treat 8b as directional, not a promise of +$4k.

—

Bottom line

  1. Setup Score isn't a usable filter.The ≥ 4 tier worked less than the 1–3 tier. Don't gate entries on it.
  2. Reversal is probably a real edge, but 78% is optimistic.Plan around ~65%, improving with size. Its losses are almost entirely same-day panic exits.
  3. The continuation "DTE lever" was a mis-read.Long contracts flag chasing an extended move, they don't cause the loss. The real filter is extension-at-entry (> 25% / 20d, > 40% off the low).
  4. Extended entries: not a lower hit-rate, a worse skew.Cap size, hard stop.
  5. Hold ~15 trading days minimum (≈ 3 weeks), ~4 weeks for reversals.
  6. Held-too-long losers all peaked ≤ +9%.If it hasn't shown you anything by ~t+10, it isn't going to — that's the time-stop, not "ride it to expiry."
  7. The counterfactual (§8).Swapping the ≤ 2-day exit for a blanket 15-day hold — losers included — takes the book from −$824 / PF 0.96 to ~+$1,240 / PF 1.14; a 20-day hold to ~+$4,170 / PF 1.35.
